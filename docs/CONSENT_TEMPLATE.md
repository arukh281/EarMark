# Earmark-Real recording consent and release form

*Template. Fill in the bracketed fields, print two copies, and keep one signed copy with the
recordings. This is not legal advice: check that it meets the data-protection law that
applies to you and to the volunteer (for example India's Digital Personal Data Protection
Act 2023, or the GDPR for volunteers in the EU or UK) before you use it.*

---

**Project:** Earmark, an open-source research project on isolating one person's voice and
detecting when that person starts speaking, so that voice assistants are not interrupted by
other voices.

**Researcher:** [name], [email], [city, country]

**Volunteer ID:** [e.g. V07] (recordings and results use this ID, never your name)

## What will be recorded

- About [10-20] minutes of you reading short prompts and speaking freely, on a laptop
  microphone and a close-talk (headset or lapel) microphone at the same time.
- A short enrolment clip (about 10 seconds) that is turned into a "voice print": a list of
  256 numbers used to recognise your voice in the recordings.
- During some takes, other sound is played or spoken in the room: a TV or podcast, a
  synthetic assistant voice from the laptop speakers, or another volunteer talking.
- The time and place of each session, and the equipment used. Nothing else about you is
  collected.

## How it will be used

- To measure how well Earmark and other systems work in real rooms (evaluation). The
  recordings are **not** used to train any model.
- Written transcripts of what you said may be made, by hand or by a speech recogniser.
- Results are published only as numbers (for example error rates), which do not identify
  you.

## Your choices (tick each one you agree to)

- [ ] **A. Evaluation.** My recordings may be used privately by the researcher to evaluate
  systems, as described above. *(Required to take part.)*
- [ ] **B. Public dataset.** My recordings, their transcripts and my volunteer ID may be
  published as part of the Earmark-Real dataset under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
  licence (CC BY 4.0). I understand that anyone could then download, copy and reuse them,
  and that published copies cannot be recalled.
- [ ] **C. Demo clips.** Up to [10] short clips (under 15 seconds each) of my voice may appear
  in the public Earmark web demo and its README.
- [ ] **D. Contact.** The researcher may contact me about follow-up recording sessions.

You may agree to A without agreeing to B, C or D.

## Your rights

- Taking part is voluntary, and there is no payment [or: a payment of [amount]].
- You may stop a session at any time without giving a reason.
- You may withdraw any consent by emailing the researcher. Recordings not yet published will
  be deleted within 30 days. Anything already published under B or C before you withdrew
  cannot be recalled from people who copied it, but it will be removed from the project's
  own pages and future releases.
- Unpublished recordings are kept on the researcher's encrypted storage and deleted by
  [date, at most 3 years from now], or sooner on request.
- Your voice print is used only inside this project. It is never used to identify you
  anywhere else, and it is never shared on its own.
